Kepler.

The one place where a marketing goal, the work done against it, and the outcome it produced are the same object.

What it is

A marketing platform for teams that have to run every channel with no specialists. Kepler builds a structured model of the brand from its website and documents, then generates and measures work across search, outbound, social and paid — all of it laddered to an account goal the user defines.

Every other tool optimises inside one channel and can tell you whether an email worked. Kepler is built to answer the question the founder actually asks: did marketing work.

Who it's for

The generalist orchestrator — the marketer of record for a lean go-to-market team or a small agency. They don't own the wings: paid may sit with an agency, content may be freelance, the CRM belongs to sales. They coordinate all of it and they answer for the number at the end.

No data team. No enterprise content stack. No operations queue. That constraint is the market position, not a limitation — see the table below.

The loop

The gap between the target and the reading is what makes generation continuous rather than episodic. An orphaned asset is correct about the brand and ignorant of what the company is trying to achieve; Kepler's generation reads the goal, the campaign, what has already been produced, and real search demand — in that order of authority.

What's in it

Structured brand model built from the website, documents and guided input. Every module reads from it.
Goals
Proposes a reachable target from the account's own rates instead of asking on a blank field.

Eight-angle variants grounded in live competitor ads, with platform limits enforced.
Social
Calendar planning, per-platform specs, on-brand carousels from the brand's own colour identity.
Measurement
Campaign-scoped tagging that survives renaming, traffic and CRM attribution, accruing history.
Change detection
Scheduled jobs that report what moved — rankings, citations, replies — not what things are.
Learning
Ratings and correction notes feed the next generation in that module. No model training required.

Why it holds

  1. It produces real output with nothing connected

    Every module works AI-only. Connecting a data source grounds that module in live data instead of estimates — an enhancement, never a dependency, and enforced in the architecture. Ten minutes to first output against a four-week implementation.

  2. The goal is a first-class object

    Competitors optimise inside a seam — activation, lifecycle messaging, production, the website. None of them holds a standing account goal that campaigns and assets ladder to and that accrues across them.

  3. It refuses to compute what it cannot defend

    No cross-channel efficiency claims. No estimate below three measured campaigns. Change events say "alongside", never "because". Share is taken over the whole picture, never the attributed slice. The workings render at full weight beside every claim — a recommendation nobody can argue with is a horoscope. These refusals are pinned by automated tests, and a competitor whose growth story depends on confident numbers cannot copy them.
